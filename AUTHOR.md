# About the Author

**Charles J. DiBella**

---

I lived outdoors on an independent, nomadic basis for over thirty years.

The journey began in 1994 through deliberate lifestyle choices, economic dislocation, and a desire for self-directed mobility. I was born in 1956. From 1994 until 2001, I lived primarily in a van throughout Arizona and New Mexico. In 2001, I set aside the vehicle and transitioned entirely to self-contained bicycle travel, starting along the Southern California coast.

That shift opened decades of continuous travel defined by complete mechanical and material self-sufficiency. I carried every piece of shelter, cooking equipment, tools, and personal belongings directly on the bicycle frame. Daily survival required wild camping, navigating severe weather extremes, and managing biological needs without reliance on institutional shelters or municipal assistance. From 2001 through 2004, I traveled along the Southern California coast before shipping the bicycle to the Big Island of Hawaii, where I lived and rode continuously from 2004 to 2007.

In 2007, I relocated to Southeast Asia and remained there through 2018, living primarily in Cambodia. During those eleven years, I provided pro bono academic coaching, English instruction, and vocational mentorship to ambitious rural and urban youth. I also co-founded student residential learning houses and cooperative living spaces to help young people overcome severe financial barriers to higher education. In 2018, I returned to long-distance touring, completing a continuous bicycle crossing of the United Kingdom and continental Europe. That was followed in 2019 by two independent solo coast-to-coast crossings of the United States. During 2020 and 2021, I returned to Cambodia to help establish an intentional community of university students, a shared residential experiment in cooperative mutual aid that continues online today.

While living in Southeast Asia, I completed two extensive bicycle expeditions through Thailand, covering several thousand kilometers across remote provinces. For lodging along those routes, I stayed overnight at Buddhist temples, known as wats. The monastic communities provided quiet grounds, fresh water, and safe shelter to an exhausted overland traveler with zero administrative demands, zero interrogation, and zero transactional hurdles. That experience offered an instructive contrast to Western municipal shelters. The temples demonstrated that offering unconditional physical sanctuary preserves personal dignity, whereas institutional systems in the United States routinely extract compliance and surrender of autonomy as the entry fee for shelter.

Returning to the United States in late 2021, I resumed overland routes through Southern California, Arizona, and New Mexico. Over the next several years, I completed several additional transcontinental coast-to-coast bicycle crossings, eventually spending extended periods in Florida with long stays centered around Gainesville and Tampa.

The long road concluded in May 2025. Entering my seventieth year with physical disabilities and medical needs required stepping off the road into stable housing and continuous healthcare management.

## Why I Wrote This

Most homelessness policy is produced by professionals who study the problem from the outside. Researchers, administrators, and consultants do valuable work, but this engineering specification originates from direct personal experience. Decades spent living outside shape distinct convictions about the structural blind spots of modern social policy.

Direct experience exposes two fatal flaws in conventional emergency shelters. Traditional programs strip people of personal sovereignty and refuse safe custody for their belongings. At the same time, these facilities impose administrative mandates and behavioral hoops that ignore biological stabilization. A human being cannot recover while their physical security is compromised and their survival gear is treated as trash.

This specification articulates those realities in an auditable engineering format. I developed these principles over many years. More recently, I collaborated with artificial intelligence systems to organize the research, verify calculations, and translate field observations into technical standards. That partnership is genuine and transparent.

## What I am Not Claiming

I am an individual describing three decades of lived experience and proposing systemic corrections. I hold no policy credentials, and no formal institution, board, or foundation funds this work. The working papers represent an open theory ready for empirical scrutiny. Every factual or fiscal assertion should be audited directly under the independent review mechanisms outlined in the governance specification.
